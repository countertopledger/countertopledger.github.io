Our six picks

Instant-read thermometer

The one tool that makes every roast, chicken and steak come out right. Look for a reading in a few seconds, a backlight and a rinse-safe body. Our pick: the TP19H (TempPro, formerly ThermoPro), which folds, rotates its display and turns on when you open it.

A good chef's knife

Many home cooks are working with a dull knife they got years ago. A sharp, comfortable 8-inch blade makes prep faster and safer. Our pick: the Victorinox Fibrox Pro, America's Test Kitchen's favorite chef's knife "for most people."

Usually $30–50Check price on Amazon

Cast iron skillet

Sears, bakes cornbread and goes from stovetop to oven, and it gets better with use. The 10.25-inch size suits one to three people. Our pick: the Lodge pre-seasoned skillet, made in the US and the default recommendation for good reason.

Usually $20–35Check price on Amazon

Rimmed half sheet pans

The workhorse for roasted vegetables, cookies and one-pan dinners. Thin, dark pans warp and burn, but heavy bare aluminum doesn't. Our pick: the Nordic Ware Naturals Baker's Half Sheet 2-pack. Hand-wash it to keep it bright.

Usually $20–35Check price on Amazon

Salad spinner

Dry greens mean dressing that sticks and herbs that last longer in the fridge. Look for a one-hand pump and a brake. Our pick: the OXO Good Grips large spinner, which America's Test Kitchen says dries greens better than any other it tested.

Usually $25–40Check price on Amazon

Rasp zester

Small, cheap and used constantly: lemon zest, a snow of parmesan, garlic and ginger without a press. Our pick: the Microplane Premium Classic, America's Test Kitchen's top zester-style rasp, with a grippy soft handle.

Usually $12–20Check price on Amazon

How to pick

  • Not sure what they own? A thermometer or a zester is the safest bet. Few kitchens have a good one, and nobody minds a spare.
  • Cooks every night? The knife or the sheet pans. They get used daily and replace worn-out versions.
  • Just moved or learning to cook? The Lodge skillet plus a thermometer covers most first recipes for roughly $35–65 together.
  • Want it to feel bigger? Pair the zester with a bag of good parmesan and a few lemons.
