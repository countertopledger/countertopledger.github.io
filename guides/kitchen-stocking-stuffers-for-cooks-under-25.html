Our six picks

A Y-peeler that actually stays sharp

Most kitchens have a peeler that drags and skips. This one glides through carrots, potatoes and even squash skin, and it costs so little that three in a stocking still feels generous. Our pick: the Kuhn Rikon Original Swiss Peeler 3-pack, which America's Test Kitchen calls "inexpensive, effective, agile, and easy to use." Hand-wash and dry it, since the blade is carbon steel.

Usually $12–20Check price on Amazon

Kitchen shears that come apart

Good shears snip herbs, trim chicken, open packages and cut pizza. Because the two blades separate, they get properly clean. Our pick: the Henckels Take-Apart Kitchen Shears, the best buy in America's Test Kitchen's shears test, which found them only "a touch less sharp and powerful" than a model that costs about three times as much.

Usually $12–22Check price on Amazon

A seamless silicone spatula

The tool a baker grabs for batter, eggs, risotto and the last bit of a jar. A one-piece spatula has no joint where food and water hide, and it handles high heat. Our pick: the Di Oro Living large seamless spatula, a co-winner in America's Test Kitchen's spatula test, which said it "handled every task with ease."

Usually $10–18Check price on Amazon

A small, sharp paring knife

For hulling strawberries, mincing a shallot or coring apples, a light 3- to 4-inch blade beats a big chef's knife. Our pick: the Victorinox Swiss Classic 3.25-inch paring knife, the best buy in America's Test Kitchen's paring knife test. It is inexpensive enough to keep one in the drawer and one in the picnic bag. Wrap the blade before it goes in a stocking.

Usually $8–15Check price on Amazon

A bench scraper

It divides bread dough, cleans flour off the counter and scoops chopped onions into the pan in one move. Our pick: the Dexter-Russell Sani-Safe 6 x 3-inch scraper, the winner of America's Test Kitchen's bench scraper test and a fixture in professional bakeries. If they mostly chop rather than bake, the OXO Good Grips scraper (ATK "Recommended") has handy ruler markings.

Usually $8–15Check price on Amazon

A mini angled measuring cup

Holds up to 4 tablespoons (2 ounces), and you can read the markings from above as you pour. It is handy for vanilla, soy sauce, oil and cocktails, and it replaces a fistful of measuring spoons. Our pick: the OXO Good Grips mini angled measuring cup, which has thousands of five-star ratings from buyers. Pair it with a bottle of good vanilla for a small, complete gift.

How we picked

We don't run a test lab, and we never accept free products or paid placements. For each pick we start from independent tests, mostly America's Test Kitchen equipment reviews, keep only items that are widely well rated by buyers and sold by reliable sellers, and check that each one usually costs under $25 and fits in a stocking. Where no independent test exists, as with the mini measuring cup, we say so and rely on a long record of buyer reviews. Price ranges come from recent price history, not from a single day.
